18 Hours of Travel, Infinite Insights: My Reflections from STC India Annual Summit 2025

Created on 2025-12-10 07:00

Published on 2025-12-10 08:13

They say the journey matters more than the destination. But when that journey involves a 9-hour travel ordeal each way, you start to question that wisdom.

I arrived at the STC India Annual Summit 2025 exhausted, having fought against delays and distance to make it to Pune. But the moment I stepped into the venue, the fatigue didn’t just fade; it was replaced by a kinetic energy that only a room full of passionate technical communicators can generate.

The summit wasn’t just a series of talks; it was a sensory experience. It was the hum of ideas colliding in the hallways, the collective nod of agreement during keynotes, and the realization that while our tools are changing, our core purpose remains the same.

Here is how the summit unfolded through my eyes (and ears).

The Vision and The Voice

The event opened with the kind of gravitas that stays with you. Dr.Sudhanshu Mani, the visionary behind the Vande Bharat Express, reminded us that the technology is cold without a human pulse. Quoting Shakespeare and Ghalib, he proved that great engineering is born from great empathy. His success wasn't just about steel and speed; it was about empowering a team to dream. Here is the Amazon.in link to his book My Train 18 Story.

From that high-level inspiration, Sachin Shenoy set the stage by challenging us to stop treating AI as an accessory. The goal isn't to be "AI-assisted" anymore; it’s to become "AI-native." It’s a subtle but powerful difference: one implies adding a turbocharger to an old engine; the other implies building a new engine entirely.

This human element echoed through, Sheece Baghdadi’s session on Voice AI. He didn't just talk about text-to-speech; he spoke poetically about the modality of voice—how audio-first AI changes the very texture of communication. Prachii Kalyani grounded this further, arguing that the "human context" is the only thing that keeps AI from becoming generic noise.

From Routine to Automation: My Two Cents

Amidst learning from others, I had the privilege of presenting my own session: "Empowering Technical Communicators with MCP Servers: From Routine Tasks to AI-Powered Automation."

My goal was to demystify the Model Context Protocol (MCP)—what I like to call the "USB-C for AI." We often struggle with M×N chaotic integrations, where every AI model needs custom code to talk to our tools (Jira, Confluence, Local Files). MCP solves this by acting as a universal adapter.

We explored how technical writers can reclaim 90% of the time spent on repetitive tasks like style guide enforcement, link checking, and creating release notes, transforming hours of manual grunt work into minutes of automation. The takeaway was simple: Every hour spent on repetitive tasks is an hour NOT spent on strategic documentation work.

The Parallel Track Dilemma

Conferences like STC India are a blessing and a curse. The blessing is the abundance of knowledge; the curse is the Parallel Track Dilemma. I missed a couple of sessions due to conflicts with my own session, but the "hallway track" - the buzz outside the doors was so loud that I felt I absorbed them by osmosis.

Two sessions, in particular, lingered in the air long after they ended.

  • The first was by Ravi Kumar Adapa. Peers told me he offered no diplomatic answers or comforting grey zones. He stripped away the fear of AI replacing us and reframed the narrative: "Learning cannot be optional anymore." The part that struck me most was his take on hallucinations. We treat AI errors as fatal flaws, but Ravi pointed out that humans "hallucinate" every day—via assumptions and biases. The future isn't about expecting perfection from AI; it’s about learning to work responsibly with imperfection, treating AI not as an overlord, but as a teammate.

  • The second was by Steev Kundukulangara. He spoke to the danger of comfort. It is easy to sit in our silos, assuming we are "fine." But events like this are designed to burst that bubble. They force us to look up, look around, and realize that the industry is moving—with or without us..

Calm Amid the Chaos

Between the high-level philosophy and the deep technical dives, the NotebookLM session by Rahul Verma and Upendra Singhai offered key insights into integrating AI tools into everyday workflows.

A Roadmap for the Future

To the entire STC India family, Paresh Naik, Rajib Borkataki, and every volunteer who surely had sleepless nights, thank you! You didn't just organize a conference; you curated an experience.

I did miss meeting a few friends due to the travel chaos, and that stings a little. The travel was exhausting. The delays were frustrating. But looking back, they were merely the toll I paid for entry. I went in curious, carrying a bag of questions. I left with a roadmap, new connections, and a realization:

The future isn't coming. It's already here. We just have to be native to it.
